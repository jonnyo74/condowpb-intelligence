## 1. The 30-second version

- **Flagler Drive stays low.** The city dropped the draft that would have allowed up to 25 stories on the waterfront. Most of the Flagler Waterfront District stays at 5 stories, and the existing 8-story incentive north of Banyan remains.
- **Height is being pushed inland.** The tallest allowances, up to 25 stories with incentives, are concentrated in the center of downtown, west of Olive, around the transit core.
- **Large projects must cut car trips, and commercial projects need less parking.** Nonresidential parking minimums drop 20% south of Banyan. Residential parking rules don't change.
- **Workforce housing rules get tougher.** Affordability periods go from 20 to 30 years, and developers who use height incentives will carry more workforce units.
- **Nothing here is law yet.** This is a proposal moving through city boards. Final votes are expected to run into early 2027.

**What this means for you:** If you own on Flagler, the biggest threat to your view and your scarcity premium just went away, at least for now. If you're buying, the new supply is coming to the core, not the waterfront, so price the two differently.

## 2. Flagler Waterfront District stays low

The first draft of the update would have let developers go from a 5-story base to as high as 25 stories along parts of the waterfront between Datura Street and Lakeview Avenue by paying into city incentive programs. After months of pushback from downtown residents, and after the mayor paused the process in July, the city removed that provision. It also dropped a proposed 12-story allowance in the Banyan-to-Datura stretch. The Downtown Action Committee voted 3–1 on Sept. 9 to move the revised plan forward.

Under the revised plan:

- **Most of the Flagler Waterfront District stays capped at 5 stories.**
- **North of Banyan Boulevard, the existing 8-story incentive remains.**

This matters more for existing owners than for anyone else. A condo on Flagler Drive is valued on three things you can't recreate: the Intracoastal view, what's in front of it, and how few buildings can ever offer the same thing. A 25-story allowance along the waterfront would have undercut all three. Keeping the district low preserves the sightlines for the towers already there, and it means new waterfront inventory will stay limited. Limited supply of a view product is what supports resale values over time.

A caveat: this is a zoning proposal, not a guarantee. Future commissions can reopen height, and projects that were already approved before the update (see Mr. C below) aren't affected by it.

Flagler Drive building guides: [[LINK: Flagler Drive building guide 1]] · [[LINK: Flagler Drive building guide 2]] · [[LINK: Flagler Drive building guide 3]] · [[LINK: Flagler Drive building guide 4]]

**What this means for you:** Owners on Flagler should read this as value protection. Your view corridor and your scarcity premium are safer than they were in the spring draft. Buyers comparing Flagler with the core should expect the waterfront premium to hold, because the city just chose not to add supply there.

## 3. Where taller buildings CAN go

The update steers height into the center of downtown, west of Olive Avenue and closer to Brightline, Tri-Rail, and the Okeechobee corridor.

| District | Boundaries | Base height | Incentive height |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transit Oriented District | Tamarind Ave. to Sapodilla Ave. | 10 stories | Up to 25 stories |
| Quadrille Garden District | Dixie Hwy. to Olive Ave. | 10 stories | Up to 15 stories |
| Sapodilla–Rosemary / Datura–Fern area | Sapodilla to Rosemary, Datura to Fern | 8 or 10 stories (varies by property) | Up to 25 stories |
| Quadrille Business District | Banyan Blvd. to Datura St., along Clematis | 10 stories | Up to 15 stories |

*Heights reflect the revised draft as of September 2026 and may change before adoption.*

**What this means for you:** If you want quiet, low density, and a protected view, look east toward Flagler. If you're buying for appreciation driven by new density (more restaurants, retail, foot traffic, and eventually higher comps as new towers set price points), look at the Transit Oriented District and the blocks around it. Expect more construction nearby for years if you do.

## 4. Downtown parks are protected

The update designates the Meyer Amphitheatre, the Great Lawn, and Jose Marti, Trinity, Howard, Post, and Gateway parks as Urban Open Space, with no height or development rights attached. That removes the long-running worry that park land along the waterfront could be traded for development rights or rolled into a future project.

**What this means for you:** The green space that makes downtown livable, and that sits in front of many Flagler Drive buildings, is locked in under this plan. Buildings facing these parks have one less long-term risk.

---

## 5. Traffic and parking

Three changes:

- **Transportation Demand Management (TDM) requirement.** Projects over 25,000 sq ft, or generating 20 or more peak-hour trips, must show how they'll cut 25% of projected peak-hour trips through shuttles, transit passes, shared parking, bike facilities, and similar measures.
- **Nonresidential parking minimums drop 20% south of Banyan.** Offices, retail, and restaurants can build less parking.
- **Residential parking requirements stay the same.** New condo buildings still have to provide the same parking as today.

The city is also putting transit behind this. **West Palm Move**, the city's electric shuttle that replaced RideWPB, runs a fixed route between Northwood Village, downtown, and the Norton Museum area every 8–10 minutes for $1 a ride, with on-demand service at $2 and free transfers between them. On the regional side, **Tri-Rail fares go up 10% on Oct. 1, 2026**, the system's first increase since 2019.

More on this: [[LINK: Downtown walkability page]]

**What this means for you:** Downtown is being set up to be more walkable and less car-dependent, which supports condo values in the core. Because residential parking rules aren't changing, your building's garage isn't getting squeezed. The trade-off is that commercial projects near you may bring less parking of their own, which can put pressure on street and public garage parking. If you commute on Tri-Rail, your costs just went up.

## 6. Workforce housing gets stricter

| Rule | Before | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Affordability period for workforce units | 20 years | 30 years |
| Share of CDR (height/density) incentive tied to workforce housing | 20% | 40% |
| Commercial projects | No requirement | Must provide workforce units off-site or pay a fee |
| Where off-site units can go | [[confirm prior rule]] | Anywhere east of I-95 |
| Rental projects: payment in lieu | Allowed | No longer allowed; units must be built |

**What this means for you:**
- **Owners:** Expect a more income-diverse downtown over time. That supports the service workers, restaurant staff, and younger professionals a real downtown depends on, and it means some new neighboring buildings will include income-restricted units.
- **Buyers:** Any new project that uses height or density incentives will carry these units. That's mostly a rental-building issue, not a condo one. If you're comparing new construction, ask whether the building used incentives and whether any units are income-restricted, because that affects resale, rental rules, and HOA makeup.

## 7. What's actually in the pipeline

> Status labels reflect public reporting as of late September 2026. Nothing below is approved unless marked. [[OWNER: confirm each status before publish]]

**120 S. Dixie Hwy. — Jeff Greene mass-timber tower** · **Status: Proposed / under review**
A 25-story apartment tower proposed under Florida's Live Local Act, which lets developers bypass some local height and density limits in exchange for workforce housing. Reported plans call for about 366 apartments, 40% of them workforce units, about 7,500 sq ft of ground-floor retail, and 236 parking spaces. The design incorporates the site's 1915 fire station at street level. If built as designed, it would be the tallest mass-timber building in Florida. **This is a rental building, not a condo.** [[VERIFY: city staff requested revisions]]

**Mr. C Hotel & Residences — 320 Lakeview Ave.** · **Status: Under construction (approved before this update)**
A 27-story tower with 110 hotel rooms and 146 branded residences with hotel-style service, from the Cipriani family's Mr. C brand. Completion is targeted for 2027. It's the clearest current example of what hotel-branded luxury condo product downtown will cost and how it will be serviced. [[VERIFY: rooftop dining]]

**25-story waterfront allowance on Flagler Drive** · **Status: Scrapped**
The draft provision that would have allowed buildings up to 25 stories along the waterfront from Datura to Lakeview, plus a 12-story allowance from Banyan to Datura, was removed in September after public backlash. These were zoning allowances, not specific building proposals.

**Save West Palm Beach — 94-project tracker and call for a pause** · **Status: Advocacy / pause rejected**
The resident group's development tracker lists 94 projects that are announced, under review, approved, under construction, or recently completed. It pushed for a two-year pause on major height, zoning, and density requests while the city studies the cumulative impact on traffic and infrastructure. Mayor Keith James rejected the pause. The group has also opposed a proposed helipad on top of the Hibiscus CityPlace garage, which the Planning Board backed and which now goes to the City Commission.

**Jaguar dealership sites — 915 S. Dixie Hwy. and 420 Okeechobee Blvd.** · **Status: Under review**
Myers Auto Group has asked the city to add its roughly 2.5-acre dealership site at Okeechobee and Dixie to the Downtown Master Plan area. That would make it the first property south of Okeechobee in the Okeechobee Business District, where heights can reach 25 stories. The Planning Board recommended inclusion, and the request goes to the City Commission. No building has been proposed.

**What this means for you:** Most of what's being built is rental or hotel-branded product, not traditional for-sale condos. That keeps resale inventory in established condo buildings relatively tight. Watch the Jaguar site closely: if it's rezoned, it becomes one of the largest high-rise sites at downtown's southern edge.

## 8. The market backdrop

Luxury home sales in West Palm Beach rose 43.9% year over year in July 2026, the largest increase of any major U.S. metro, according to Redfin data reported by The Real Deal. Pending luxury sales were up 20.1%, and the median luxury sale price was about $4.5 million, up 7.5%. The driver is the same one behind the master plan debate: finance firms and their executives keep relocating here, turning the area into what's now routinely called "Wall Street South." More high-earning households chasing a fixed supply of waterfront product is exactly why the height fight on Flagler mattered.

[[VERIFY against the Real Deal article: exact wording "fastest growth in the country" and "Wall Street South"; note that 43.9% is a single-month (July) figure, and that it covers all luxury homes, not condos specifically]]

**What this means for you:** Demand at the top of the market is strong. Owners of true luxury waterfront units are sitting on the strongest hand. Buyers shouldn't assume they can wait out prices, but they also shouldn't treat a single month of luxury home data as a condo forecast. Look at building-level comps.

## 9. Buyer FAQ

*(Mark up with FAQPage JSON-LD; see schema below.)*

**Will my Flagler view get blocked?**
Under the revised plan, it's much less likely. The 25-story waterfront allowance was removed, most of the Flagler Waterfront District stays at 5 stories, and north of Banyan the cap remains the existing 8-story incentive. Projects approved before the update, and Live Local Act projects, follow different rules, so check what's approved on the specific parcels in front of your unit.

**Is downtown getting more or less walkable?**
More. The plan adds trip-reduction requirements for large projects, lowers nonresidential parking minimums south of Banyan, and pairs with the $1 West Palm Move shuttle connecting Northwood, downtown, and the Norton Museum area every 8–10 minutes.

**Should I wait for new construction?**
Most of the new downtown supply in the pipeline is rental or hotel-branded, not traditional condos, and new towers will take years to deliver. If you want a waterfront view, the plan limits future supply, so waiting doesn't produce more options. If you want the core, new product will come, but at new-construction prices.

**Where do the workforce units go?**
On-site in incentive-using projects, or off-site anywhere east of I-95 for commercial projects that choose off-site units instead of paying the fee. Rental projects can no longer pay a fee instead of building the units.

**Does this change HOA or assessment risk?**
Not directly. The master plan governs zoning, not building finances. Your assessment risk is still driven by building age, reserves, and Florida's post-Surfside structural and reserve requirements. Indirectly, construction next door can mean disruption, and new competing product can affect rental demand. [[LINK: condo inspection/reserves guide, if one exists]]
